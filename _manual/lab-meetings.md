## Lab meetings

Lab members are expected to attend weekly lab meetings and one-on-one meetings. Please make an effort to arrive on time, but if a participant session or other unforeseen event causes a delay, notify the team (for lab meetings) or Ashley (for one-on-one meetings) as soon as possible. Use Slack to communicate if you will be late or need to reschedule.

**Lab or Team Meetings**
Our lab holds two regular weekly group meetings, which most or all lab members are expected to attend:

1.	_**Scientific Lab Meeting**_: This 1-hour meeting is held weekly and open to all lab members, including both undergraduate and graduate students. The meeting is divided into two parts. The first 15 minutes are dedicated to addressing any problems, concerns, or updates related to recruitment or participants. The remaining 45 minutes focus on topics of scientific relevance to the lab, which may involve reviewing journal articles, presenting ongoing research, or collaborating to troubleshoot methodological or theoretical challenges. At the start of each semester, lab members will sign up to lead specific sessions. These meetings will continue year-round, including during summer breaks, though the frequency may be adjusted around holidays. These meetings will be held in-person in the conference/testing area of the lab (room 616B). However, if a team member is unable to attend in person but can do so virtually, a Zoom option will be available.

2.	_**Graduate Student Lab Meeting**_: This 1-hour meeting is held every other week and is dedicated to larger lab management and projects, graduate student-specific projects, and senior lab member level discussions. Agenda items may include participant recruitment, staff-related issues, reviewing or creating standard operating procedures, discussions about available resources and lab finances, and other topics relevant to graduate-level lab operations.

These meetings help ensure both the scientific progress and efficient functioning of the lab.

**One-on-One Individual Graduate Student Meetings**
I hold regular one-on-one meetings with all graduate student lab members to ensure that you receive the guidance and support you need. These meetings are designed to focus on your needs, whether that’s feedback on research, career development, or discussing any challenges you might face. My goal is to make myself available to you, so please use these meetings as a dedicated time to get what you need from me as your mentor.

Here’s how you can help make these meetings productive:

•	<u>Be on time</u>: If you’re running late or need to reschedule, just let me know as soon as you can, and I’ll do the same for you.

•	Come prepared: Bring any questions, ideas, or data you’d like to discuss. You don’t need a formal document, but having an agenda or a list of topics helps keep the meeting focused. If we’re reviewing data, bring it in a format that’s easy to look at together (e.g., PowerPoint/Keynote).

•	Send materials ahead: If there’s a specific piece of writing (like a paper or grant) you want feedback on, send it to me at least three business days in advance. For longer documents (over five pages), please provide at least a week.
•	Share personal challenges: If life outside the lab is affecting your work, you don’t need to share details but do let me know. We can work together to find ways to support your mental health while staying productive.

•	After the meeting: Feel free to send a brief summary of our discussion and any action items we agreed on. This helps keep us both accountable and aligned on the next steps.

The frequency of one-on-one meetings may vary based on your role or career stage. Graduate students in their first year should plan for weekly check-ins, while more senior students may meet less frequently, depending on their level of independence. Meetings with staff and post-docs will be scheduled based on individual needs.

These meetings are meant to be a supportive space for your growth, not a source of stress. If there’s nothing urgent to discuss, it’s okay to cancel a meeting, but be mindful that regular cancellations might indicate a need for us to check in.

**Project- or Topic-Specific Meetings**
Project-specific meetings will be arranged as necessary to address the unique needs of individual studies. These meetings may be scheduled for collaborations with other labs, for internal projects that involve only a subset of lab members, or for discussions with key stakeholders in new initiatives. The goal is to ensure that relevant team members have focused time to discuss the project, troubleshoot challenges, and coordinate next steps. These meetings will be scheduled based on the requirements of the project and may occur on a regular or ad-hoc basis.

**As-Needed Meetings**
In addition to regular and project-specific meetings, as-needed meetings can be scheduled to address urgent issues or specific topics requiring dedicated attention. Lab members are strongly encouraged to request these meetings whenever a problem arises or when additional discussion would help move a project forward more efficiently.
Examples of as-needed meetings include:

•	Problem-solving sessions: If an unexpected challenge occurs, whether it’s research-related or logistical, it’s encouraged to schedule a one-on-one or group meeting to address the issue promptly.
•	Project launch meetings: When starting a new project, these meetings provide time to align on key procedures, train team members, and ensure everyone is coordinated for the project’s success.
•	Data-specific discussions: For in-depth conversations about data collection, analysis, or interpretation for manuscripts, these meetings can be scheduled separately from regular lab meetings to allow adequate time for review.

These meetings are meant to provide flexibility and ensure that any issue or new development is addressed in a timely and productive manner. To set up an as-needed meeting, please email me at ashley.sanders@uga.edu. 

**Ad hoc Communication**
I recognize that sometimes you may need quick, unplanned input or advice to keep your projects moving forward. While I may not always be immediately available, I want to ensure that you feel comfortable seeking guidance as needed.
If you need immediate feedback or have an urgent question, I encourage you to reach out. When my office door is open, feel free to stop by for a quick chat. However, if I'm not available, you can contact me via Slack or email, and I’ll do my best to respond promptly. Please note that depending on my schedule, it may be more efficient to schedule a short follow-up meeting to ensure we have time to address your needs thoroughly.
While I prioritize being available for support, I also encourage you to troubleshoot independently or collaborate with peers. Sometimes, a fresh perspective from a fellow lab member can be just as helpful. If I’m unable to help immediately, I may suggest alternative approaches or direct you to others in the lab who can assist.
